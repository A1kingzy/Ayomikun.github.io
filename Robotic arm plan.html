Violating them will cause assembly failure, structural weakness, or servo damage. 📐 Servo Mounting Geometry Miuzei 9g servo body: 22.8mm × 12.5mm × 22mm (L×W×H). Add 0.3mm clearance on all sides for insertion. DS3218MG body: 40.5mm × 20mm × 38mm. Significantly larger — design the base bracket accordingly. Servo mounting screws: M2 × 10mm through the servo ears. Ears are 4mm wide with 2.2mm holes. Horn-to-link connection: Design a recess/socket for the horn so rotation is constrained by the link geometry, not just the horn screw. 25T spline shaft diameter: 5.8mm — design any through-holes in links to match. 🧱 Print Settings & Material Rules Infill: Minimum 40% for structural links, 20% acceptable for covers/aesthetics. Infill pattern: Gyroid or honeycomb — superior strength-to-weight vs lines. Wall count: Minimum 4 perimeters on all load-bearing parts. Layer height: 0.2mm for structural parts, 0.15mm for fine holes and servo pockets. Orientation: Print links so layer lines run along the length — avoid transverse layers at high-stress bends. Heat-set insert holes: Use 4.2mm holes for M3 inserts in PETG (slightly undersized; the insert melts in). No support required: Design link geometry with ≤45° overhangs or add built-in chamfers/bridges. 📏 Link Length & Torque Constraints Upper arm (shoulder → elbow): Maximum 150mm. Beyond this, the 20KG servo torque becomes marginal with a full payload. Forearm (elbow → wrist): Maximum 120mm. Uses 9g servo at elbow — keep it light. Wrist to gripper tip: Maximum 80mm. Every mm here multiplies the torque demand on the wrist servo. Torque budget check: Torque needed = (mass × distance from joint). At 150mm reach with 200g payload = 0.03 N·m. The 20KG servo provides ~2 N·m — plenty of margin. Weight target: Each printed link should be under 80g. Weigh before assembly. 🔩 Base Design Requirements Base must hold a 608ZZ bearing (22mm OD, 8mm ID) for smooth rotation. Design a 22.2mm press-fit pocket. Base footprint minimum: 120mm × 120mm for stability. Include 4× M4 mounting holes on 100mm spacing. Base must be bolted to a surface — the arm torque will tip it over if left free. Design for M4 bolts into a wooden base plate. Cable routing: Include a 12mm channel through the base for wiring to pass through the rotation axis. 🤏 Gripper / Scraper Tool Constraints Gripper mechanism: Use a single 9g servo driving a 4-bar linkage or rack-and-pinion for parallel jaw motion. Jaw opening: Design for 0–60mm spread. Most prints fit within this range. Scraper blade slot: 80mm × 1.5mm slot in one jaw to hold a putty knife blade for prying prints. Jaw material: Print TPU on the gripping faces OR glue a rubber pad. Hard PETG jaws will crush/mark prints. Clearance from print bed: Gripper tip must reach within 2mm of the print bed when the arm is fully extended. 🔌 Electronics Enclosure Constraints ESP32 footprint: 51mm × 28mm. Needs 1mm clearance on USB side for programming access. PCA9685 footprint: 62mm × 25mm. Buck converters: XL4016 module: ~75mm × 45mm. Mini buck: ~22mm × 17mm. Enclosure must be separate from arm — do not mount electronics in a moving link. Place at base or on a fixed side panel. Ventilation slots: XL4016 gets warm under load — add 2mm slots above and below the module. LiPo bay: Design a slide-in tray with XT60 socket. Allow 5mm extra on all sides of the battery pack (3S 3000mAh is typically 105mm × 35mm × 25mm). 6 Wiring & Power Diagrams Power Distribution Diagram ╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗ ║ POWER DISTRIBUTION (PORTABLE) ║ ╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝ [3S LiPo 11.1V] ──XT60──► MAIN SWITCH │ ┌───────────────────┤ │ │ [XL4016 Buck] [Mini Buck] IN: 11.1V IN: 11.1V OUT: 6.0V 10A OUT: 5.0V 1A │ │ │ └──► ESP32 VIN pin │ ESP32 GND pin │ └──► PCA9685 V+ (6V servo power) └──► PCA9685 GND │ ├── CH0 PWR ──► DS32181MG Base (RED + BROWN wires) ├── CH1 PWR ──► DS3218MG Shoulder ├── CH2 PWR ──► 9g Elbow ├── CH3 PWR ──► 9g Wrist Pitch ├── CH4 PWR ──► 9g Wrist Roll └── CH5 PWR ──► 9g Gripper ALL GNDs TIED TOGETHER: LiPo GND = Buck GND = ESP32 GND = PCA GND ESP32 ↔ PCA9685 I2C Wiring ╔════════════════════════════════════════════════╗ ║ I2C CONTROL WIRING ║ ╚════════════════════════════════════════════════╝ ESP32 Pin PCA9685 Pin ───────── ─────────── GPIO 21 (SDA) ──► SDA GPIO 22 (SCL) ──► SCL GND ──► GND 3.3V ──► VCC (logic power ONLY) │ 6V Buck Rail ──► V+ (servo motor power) ⚠ VCC and V+ are DIFFERENT pins on the PCA9685 ⚠ VCC = logic (3.3V from ESP32) ⚠ V+ = servo power (6V from Buck) Servo Connector Pinout ╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════╗ ║ SERVO 3-PIN CONNECTOR (Standard) ║ ╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════╝ PIN 1 (BROWN/BLACK) ──► GND PIN 2 (RED) ──► V+ (6V from PCA9685 power rail) PIN 3 (ORANGE/YELL) ──► PWM Signal (from PCA9685 channel) PCA9685 channel header (3 pins per channel): [ GND | V+ | PWM ] ← in order on the board Decoupling Capacitor Placement Place 1000µF caps across power rails at 3 points: 1. Observe polarity! 7 Step-by-Step Build Guide Phase 1: Design & Print (Week 1–2) 1 Measure your servos precisely With calipers, measure your actual Miuzei servos — stated dimensions vary. Note the exact ear hole spacing, body width, and horn spline diameter. These are your CAD ground truth. 2 Design base + base bracket Model the base plate (120×120mm footprint), the 608ZZ bearing housing, and the DS3218MG mount. This is the most critical part — print a test fit before printing the final version. 3 Design upper arm + shoulder bracket 150mm link with DS3218MG at the shoulder joint. Include cable routing channel (6mm diameter) running through the length. 4 Design forearm + elbow 120mm link driven by a 9g servo at the elbow. Keep this link as light as possible — use gyroid infill at 30%. 5 Design wrist assembly + gripper Two 9g servos for pitch and roll. Gripper uses one 9g servo with a 4-bar linkage. Include slot for scraper blade. 6 Print all parts in PETG Print base parts at 40% gyroid infill, 4 walls. Print forearm/wrist at 30% infill. Do a dry assembly with no screws before committing to final print — check all fits. Phase 2: Electronics (Week 3) 7 Configure buck converters before connecting anything Use a multimeter. Set the XL4016 to exactly 6.0V and the mini buck to 5.0V using the trim potentiometer. Do this with NO servos or ESP32 connected. 8 Wire and test PCA9685 + ESP32 Connect I2C (GPIO21/22). Power ESP32 from 5V buck. Upload Adafruit PCA9685 library test sketch. Verify you can address the board (I2C scanner should find 0x40). 9 Test each servo individually Connect one servo at a time to CH0. Sweep 0°–180°. Listen for grinding (wrong voltage) or stalling (mechanical bind). Test all 7 servos before assembly. 10 Install capacitors Solder 1000µF caps at the 3 locations described in the wiring section. Observe polarity. Power cycle with all servos connected and watch for voltage sag on multimeter — should stay above 5.7V. Phase 3: Mechanical Assembly (Week 4) 11 Install heat-set inserts Use soldering iron at ~220°C for PETG. Press inserts flush while hot. Let cool completely before threading screws — they will be rock solid. 12 Press-fit bearings into base Use a vice or clamp — never hammer. Bearings should be snug with no wobble. If too tight, sand the pocket lightly. 13 Centre all servos before mounting Send 90° (1500µs pulse) to every servo before installing. Mark the horn at 90°. This gives you a known zero position to calibrate from. 14 Assemble arm from base up Base → shoulder → upper arm → elbow → forearm → wrist → gripper. Route wires through channels at each stage. Do not close cable channels until all servos tested. 15 Bolt arm to wooden base plate Use M4 bolts through the 100mm spacing holes into a 20mm thick wooden board. The arm will tip without this. Optionally add rubber feet to the board. Phase 4: Software (Week 5–6) 16 Implement Bluetooth serial on ESP32 Use BluetoothSerial library. Create a command parser: "MOVE:x,y,z", "GRIP:open", "GRIP:close", "HOME". Test with a serial Bluetooth terminal app on the tablet. 17 Implement inverse kinematics For a 3-DOF planar arm, IK is just trigonometry. Given target (x,y,z), compute shoulder and elbow angles using the law of cosines. Test with known positions before integrating with the printer workflow. 18 Build Android tablet app Use MIT App Inventor (no coding needed) or a simple Kotlin/Java app. Connect to OctoPrint REST API to watch for print_done event, then send Bluetooth command to ESP32. Add manual override buttons. 19 Calibrate removal trajectory Manually jog the arm to the print bed position and record the joint angles. Define a sequence: approach → grip → lift → move to bin → release. Save these as named positions. 20 Full system test Run 5 full print-removal cycles. Monitor LiPo voltage (should not drop below 10.5V). Check all joints for heat buildup. Adjust gripper pressure if prints are being crushed or dropped. 8 Software Architecture ESP32 Firmware Stack BluetoothSerial — receive commands from tablet Adafruit PCA9685 — PWM servo driver library Custom IK solver — 3-DOF geometric solution State machine — IDLE / MOVING / GRIPPING / HOMING Command parser — parse "MOVE:x,y,z" strings Android Tablet App OctoPrint API client — poll print status every 5s Bluetooth manager — maintain connection to ESP32 Manual controls — override joystick and buttons Safety lockout — prevent arm movement while printing Log viewer — show last 20 removal events Command Protocol (Tablet → ESP32 over Bluetooth) Command Format: "CMD:params " MOVE:x,y,z → Move end effector to position (mm from base) JOINT:j,deg → Set joint j (0-5) to absolute angle in degrees GRIP:open → Open gripper to max GRIP:close → Close gripper (gentle force) HOME → Return all joints to safe home position STATUS → ESP32 replies with joint angles + state REMOVE → Execute full print removal sequence Response Format: "OK:data " or "ERR:message " Print Removal State Machine [IDLE] │ tablet sends REMOVE command ▼ [APPROACH] → arm moves to safe approach position above bed │ ▼ [DESCEND] → lower to bed level, gripper open │ ▼ [GRIP] → close gripper around print edge │ ▼ [PEEL] → slight rocking motion to break bed adhesion │ ▼ [LIFT] → raise arm with print │ ▼ [DEPOSIT] → rotate to bin position, open gripper, drop │ ▼ [HOME] → return to neutral position, signal "DONE" to tablet │ ▼ [IDLE] 9 Safety & LiPo Guidelines 🔥 LiPo Batteries Can Catch Fire If Mishandled LiPo batteries can cause fires if over-discharged, overcharged, punctured, or short-circuited. These are not scare tactics — read and follow every point below. Charging Rules Always use a proper balance charger (IMAX B6 or equivalent) — never a generic charger. Charge at 1C rate maximum — for a 3000mAh pack, that's 3A max. Never leave charging unattended or overnight. Fully charged 3S voltage = 12.6V. If charger stops at this, it's done. Charge in a LiPo-safe bag or metal tin as a precaution. Discharge Rules Never discharge below 3.5V per cell = 10.5V total for 3S. The LiPo alarm plugged into the balance lead will beep when voltage drops — stop immediately. If a cell goes below 3.0V, the pack may be permanently damaged. Storage Rules Store at storage voltage: 3.8V per cell = 11.4V for 3S. Most chargers have a "storage" mode. Store in a cool, dry place — not in the arm or near a heat source. Check voltage monthly if not in use. Mechanical Safety Always home the arm before powering down — servos lose position on power loss. Disconnect power before working on any wiring.
